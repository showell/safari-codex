================================================================================
A. DEFECTS AND HOLES
================================================================================

A1. real-to-int and real-from-int have no zig emitter.        [HOLE - FIXED]

    "error: zig plug: no emitter for real-to-int" (and real-from-int). Both
    confirmed missing independently.

    Consequences, in order of how much they cost:
      - Gpu chapter DeviceMath is PARTLY dark: real-sin and real-cos are, because
        dm-reduce calls real-from-int. The port carries port/Trig.codex as a
        stand-in for exactly that.
        CORRECTED 2026-08-29, and the correction is worth having. This bullet
        used to read "DeviceMath CANNOT BE TRANSPILED AT ALL", which is wrong,
        because emission is per-function and not per-chapter (B6). real-sqrt
        does NOT call dm-reduce -- it is a scaled Heron iteration and touches no
        conversion at all -- so a chapter that cites DeviceMath and reaches only
        real-sqrt transpiles, builds and runs TODAY. Measured by doing it:
        port/GuardRail.codex cites Gpu chapter DeviceMath for its post-direction
        square root and is graded green against the zig. The stand-in's footprint
        is therefore smaller than this file claimed: Trig owes upstream sine and
        cosine, and nothing else.
      - No counted loop can widen its index. Every `i / n * two-pi` becomes a
        Real accumulator threaded alongside the Integer counter. Three sites in
        the port so far (Tree's cone ring, Scene's ridge, Scene's canopy).
      - Combined with A2 below, a transpiled program CANNOT REPORT A COMPUTED
        REAL AT ALL. That is the expensive one — see the note under A2.

    The fix looks small: each is one ZigBuiltinEmitter table row plus one prelude
    helper, the shape of bits-to-real-approx at ZigEmitter.codex:1036 and :3784.
    Bare metal is cvttsd2si / cvtsi2sd (X86_64Builtins.codex:1663-1679) —
    truncate toward zero, INT64_MIN on NaN or overflow. Zig's @intFromFloat
    truncates the same way but is UB out of range, so the honest emission is a
    guarded prelude helper, not a bare cast.

    CLOSED 2026-08-29. It was that small, and the estimate above was accurate.
    cobblestone-realconv a2a2f6f4, "zig plug: emit real-to-int and real-from-int".
    Two table rows, two prelude parts, two reserved names. The guards match the
    hardware over 31 values checked by inline asm — zero disagreements — and the
    transpiler's fixed point holds byte-identically across the change. Full
    account in PLUG_WORK.md; upstream row is plugs-backlog 2.03. NOT YET SENT.

    The two counted-loop consequences are retired with it: a Real accumulator no
    longer has to be threaded beside an Integer counter, and Tree's cone ring,
    Scene's ridge and Scene's canopy could each drop theirs. They have NOT been
    rewritten — the ports are graded green as they stand and there is no reason
    to churn them, but a new module need not pay that any more.

    AND DEVICEMATH IS NOW FULLY CLEAR. The correction above said only real-sin
    and real-cos were dark, via dm-reduce. dm-reduce is exactly what real-to-int
    and real-from-int unblock, so nothing in that chapter is dark any more --
    checked, not assumed: citing it and calling real-sin 100.0 transpiles, builds
    and answers -0.506, which means the reduction over sixteen turns is running.
    port/Trig.codex is therefore RETIRABLE. It has not been retired, and that is
    a hold rather than an oversight: Trig's wrap subtracts in a bounded loop
    where dm-reduce divides and converts back through the integers, so the two
    disagree in the last bits, and four graded chapters cite Trig today. It is
    its own change with its own regrade.

    STILL DARK, and unchanged by this: real-approx-* (the f32 family). Filling
    those would make the house-wide f32-computed-as-f64 collapse observable for
    the first time, which is section 6's conversation and not this one.

A2. `show` on a Real is refused — deliberately, and the refusal is right, but
    the CONSEQUENCE is worth stating.               [BY DESIGN - COST RETIRED]

    @compileError("zig plug: show on a Real needs __real_to_text, which this plug
    does not have"). std.fmt would agree with bare metal on some values and not
    others, so it refuses rather than guess. Correct.

    But with A1 also missing there is NO WAY OUT for a Real: it cannot be
    printed, and it cannot be converted to something that can. A failing test can
    say WHERE it failed and not WHAT it computed.

    Measured cost, twice this session:
      - Calibrating the camera tolerance meant BINARY-SEARCHING it (tighten,
        rebuild, see which seams go red) instead of reading an error.
      - A red coordinate in the tree port had to be diagnosed by re-simulating
        the whole cone in f64 in a separate zig program, to prove that
        20.606868 vs the gold's 20.606842 was width and not a bug.
    Landing A1 retires both: real-to-int (x * 1000.0) gives scaled-integer dumps
    and plain diffs.

    AND IT DID, 2026-08-29. The refusal on `show` still stands and is still
    right — nothing here argues with it — but a Real is no longer trapped:

        scaled : Real -> Integer
        scaled (x) = real-to-int (x * 1000.0)

        scaled 3.14159    ->  3141
        scaled -2.71828   -> -2718

    Grade can grow the scaled-integer dump its own prose anticipated, and the
    tolerance arguments can start reporting what they actually saw. Neither has
    been done yet; the grader is unchanged and every seam still reports an index.

A3. OvError silently becomes a WRAPPING multiply.                    [DEFECT]

    Codex declares int-ty-default : CodexType = IntegerTy i64-min i64-max
    OvError (Types/CodexType.codex:129). The zig plug emits `*%`.

        a * a   where a = 4000000000
        ->  -2446744073709551616      exit 0, no diagnostic

    Exactly the i64 wrap. Same family as the trapping/saturating collapse at
    ZigEmitter.codex:1270-1273. This is a silent wrong answer, and it is the
    single reason a fixed-point port of this game was rejected: its whole
    technique is large intermediate products.
    Repro: price-b/ovf2.codex.

A4. Math chapter Cordic is four times less accurate than it claims, and its
    test never calls it.                                             [DEFECT]

    Docstring: "16 iterations gives ~0.1% accuracy." Measured worst error 0.45%
    (cordic-sin 300 = 291 against a true 295.52). Its atan table
    [785,463,244,124,62,31,15,7,3,1,0,0,0,0,0,0] saturates — the last six
    iterations are no-ops.

    Upstream's gate cannot see it: codex/test/forewords/math-cordic.expected is
    the single line "Math/Cordic OK", and the test body only prints that string.
    Nothing anywhere calls a Cordic function.
    Repro: price-b/cordic_probe.codex.

A5. A multi-line application inside a `let` emits @panic AS A CALLEE, with no
    diagnostic.                                                      [DEFECT]

    This one is worth a careful look, because the same shape is DIAGNOSED
    elsewhere (CDX1070, item B1) and here it is not.

        let pts =
          [ ScreenPt { ... }, ... ]
        in ...

    emitted, for the value of pts:

        b4: { const _f4 = @panic("\x01"); break :b4 _f4.call(_f4.ctx, <the list>); }

    The list was parsed as an ARGUMENT to something the emitter had no value for,
    so it emitted a panic where the callee goes and then called it. Codegen
    reported no error; zig then rejected it as unreachable code, which is the
    only reason it was noticed. Putting the literal on one line fixes it.

    A program that failed this way in an expression zig happened to accept would
    be a runtime panic with no source-level explanation.

A6. Single-letter function names a, b, c, d are unusable.            [DEFECT]

    A Codex function named `d` emits `fn d_`, which collides with the Tup4
    comptime parameter `d_`:
        error: function parameter shadows declaration of 'd_'
    Found by accident naming a one-line helper `d`. Cheap to hit, cheap to fix,
    and a mangling collision rather than anything deep.

A7. Real is f64 in every plug; bare metal computes f32 in f32.       [KNOWN]

    Not found here — recorded in NOTES.txt section 6 and re-confirmed. Listed so
    this file is complete: ZigEmitter.codex:297 and :328 map every RealTy to
    f64 whatever width the type asked for, and 1270-1273 collapse the approx /
    trapping / saturating opcodes onto one operator, so TRAPPING does not trap
    and SATURATING does not saturate. The C# plug is character-for-character
    identical and the wasm plug states it as policy, so this is a house-wide
    convention and NOT a zig quirk — ask before patching. The f32 conversion
    builtins are all missing too, which is why the collapse has never been
    observable from this arm: the hole hides the wrong answer.


================================================================================
B. CORRECT BUT SURPRISING — read before writing Codex
================================================================================

B1. CDX1070 — an application may not span a newline.
    "Application ended at newline; '[' on line N is not parsed as an argument."
    Wrap the application in parentheses, bind it with a let, or put the arguments
    on one line. Cost two builds. See A5 for the case where it is NOT diagnosed.

B2. CDX1023 — a `let` nested inside an `if` arm inside a `let` is refused.
    "Expected 'in' after let bindings." Flatten it: hoist the inner computation
    into its own function. Doing so in Geom's clip-near EXPOSED A REAL BUG of
    mine, so the refusal earned its keep.

B3. CDX2085 — `==` between Reals is refused, and CODEX IS RIGHT.
    "Floating-point equality is not safe. Use the ~ operator: x ~ y
    (approximately equal, 4 ULP tolerance), x ~0 y (bitwise exact, if you are
    certain)." zig writes a plain == and says nothing.

    This forced a real decision in tree.zig's hull sort. The zig's `a.x == b.x`
    is bitwise, so the port needs ~0. A 4-ULP ~ would have called distinct ring
    points equal, reordered the sort, and changed the hull's POINT COUNT — a
    structural error that a coordinate tolerance would never have caught.
    Best single argument in this file for Codex over zig.

B4. CDX2001 — no implicit Integer to Real widening.
    `0.0 + i` is a type error, not a widening. With A1 missing there is no
    conversion either, so carry a Real accumulator beside the Integer counter.

B5. CDX3001 — chapters share ONE FLAT NAMESPACE inside a bundle.
    Citing Math chapter Geometry for its convex-hull collided with the port's own
    Geom: "Duplicate type definition: 'Vec3' is already defined", 21 errors.
    Citing a foreword chapter is not free of a port's own type names. The hull
    was ported instead, which is more faithful anyway.

B6. The emitter emits only what the entry can REACH, and silently.
    A first attempt at the scene chapter had an `opening` that referenced nothing
    in it. Result: exit 0, no diagnostic, and a ZERO-BYTE program. If a chapter
    vanishes, check that opening actually touches it.

B7. `Integer between lo and hi clamping` is real and type-enforced.
    From arith.codex: Score { v = 250 } with `v : Integer between 0 and 100
    clamping` yields 100. Worth remembering for colour channels.

B8. Integer division truncates toward zero, both signs.
    -7/2 = -3, 7/-2 = -3. Matches zig @divTrunc. There is no floor division.

B9. B6's reachability rule CUTS BOTH WAYS, and the useful direction is the one
    nobody had tried: a chapter with an unemittable function in it is not itself
    unemittable. B6 records the cost -- an unreached chapter silently vanishes.
    The benefit is the same mechanism: cite a chapter for the functions you CAN
    emit and the ones you cannot are never reached, so they are never emitted and
    never diagnosed.

    This is how the port got a real square root. Gpu chapter DeviceMath was
    written off wholesale (see the correction under A1) on the strength of
    dm-reduce needing real-from-int. But real-sqrt is independent of it:

        real-sqrt (x) = if x <= 0.0 then 0.0 else dm-sqrt-scaled x 1.0 700

    -- a scale by fours into [0.25, 4) and five Newton steps, all in Real. Citing
    DeviceMath and touching only real-sqrt transpiles clean and prints the right
    answers. Took four minutes to test and retired a stand-in that would otherwise
    have grown a third function.

    THE RULE TO CARRY: before writing a stand-in for a foreword chapter, check
    which of ITS functions actually reach the hole. "This chapter is dark" is
    almost never true; "these functions are dark" usually is.

B12. AN ALIAS-SHAPED DEFINITION IS INLINED AND IS NOT THERE TO CALL.

    A Codex definition whose body is a single application does not become a zig
    function. Six written for the wasm shim's convenience --

        scene-sun-x-at (u) = (scene-sun-at u).x

    -- emitted nothing at all; `report`, their only Codex caller, had
    `scene_sun_at(u_).x` inlined into it, and the wasm build failed on six
    undeclared identifiers.

    This only bites at the boundary, and that is the thing to remember: inside
    Codex an inlined alias is invisible and harmless, but the SHIM IS OUTSIDE THE
    PROGRAM and can call only what survives. Write the shim against the real
    functions -- `sky-color`, `sun-pos` -- rather than against wrappers, and drop
    wrappers that exist purely to be called from zig.

    Note the family resemblance to B6: both are the emitter declining to emit
    something, silently, and both surface as a missing name downstream rather than
    as a diagnostic.

B11. A CODEX KEYWORD USED AS A LET BINDING IS DIAGNOSED AS A MISSING `in`.

    mountains.zig normalises a fraction it calls `above`. Porting that name
    verbatim halts codegen with

        CDX1023 Expected 'in' after let bindings

    over a `let` that has its `in`, on the line above the real problem. `above` is
    a KEYWORD -- Lexer.codex:333, `else if w == "above" then AboveKeyword`, sitting
    with the proof-language set beside `cites`, `claim`, `proof`, `qed` and
    `induction`. The refusal is right; the message names the wrong construct and
    does not mention the keyword or its position.

    Cost a bisect: no error line is reported anywhere, codexzig writes only the
    HALT summary, so the chapter had to be truncated repeatedly to find which
    definition carried it. Watch for it whenever a zig identifier is copied
    across -- `above` is an ordinary word in graphics code and not an obvious
    keyword. Renaming to `above-frac` is the whole fix.

B10. B3's ~0 comes up again, and the second instance is more dangerous than the
    first. guard_rail.zig guards a division by a run length:

        if (len == 0) len = 1;

    Codex refuses the bare == (CDX2085) and the caller must choose. It is ~0,
    bitwise, because the zig's == is. Choosing the 4-ULP ~ instead would fire the
    fallback on path points that are merely CLOSE, swinging that post from its own
    run direction to the fixed one -- a post visibly rotated on screen, not a
    rounding difference. Unlike the hull case in B3 it would not change any point
    count, so the structural half of the judge would not catch it: only the
    coordinate gate would, and only because the error is large.


================================================================================
C. ENVIRONMENT
================================================================================

C1. codexzig writes the ZIG TO STDERR and diagnostics to stdout.
    ON A HALT IT WRITES THE REASON TO STDERR TOO — so the output file is short
    and NON-EMPTY rather than empty, and a `[ -s ]` test calls that success. This
    cost a confusing failure; harness/build_wasm.sh now greps for the prelude
    marker instead.

C2. A transpiled program prints to STDERR.
    print-line-uni becomes std.debug.print. Every harness here reads stderr.

C3. Codex has no mutable globals.
    camera.zig's `pub var view_w` is threaded as a parameter in the port. It is
    the camera's only mutable state.

C4. Codex has no libm, and its foreword is mostly INTEGER fixed point.
    There are no transcendental builtins at all — only conversions and the
    arithmetic modes. DeviceMath is a 78-line hand-written Codex chapter, and it
    is one of only ten files in the whole foreword that use Real. Cordic,
    Easing, Color, GameCamera and Numeric are all Integer milli-units.

C5. wasm32-freestanding is THREE PRELUDE FUNCTIONS away, not the gulf NOTES
    section 5 describes. The entry spawns a thread purely for a 512 MB stack; the
    heap reserves 4 GiB through page_allocator, which is the whole of a wasm32
    address space; printing reaches std.Io and drags in posix. Nothing in the
    TRANSPILED PROGRAM needs changing. See harness/wasmify.py.

C8. A PER-FRAME PURE PROGRAM NEEDS AN ARENA RESET, AND THE SHIM IS THE PLACE.

    C6 says the bump heap never reclaims. In a browser that is not a slow leak, it
    is an immediate death: a blitter calls renderFrame() sixty times a second, and
    the heap is gone in under a second. Measured across the history, every build
    of the proof of concept:

        the rail-era build (7008fce)      42 frames    0.7s at 60fps
        the sky-era build  (9b6d6a5)      42 frames    0.7s
        with the real mountain ranges      1 frame     immediately
        with the arena reset          200,001 frames   55 minutes, still exact

    THE PAGE HAD NEVER WORKED FOR MORE THAN A SECOND, and nobody knew, because
    blitter.js renders up to thirty warm-up frames behind a spinner before it
    reveals the canvas -- so the failure looked like a spinner and then a dead
    canvas, which reads as a build problem rather than a heap one. It cost real
    eye-testing time and was found only by accident, while adding a scrub control.

    THE GATE COULD NOT SEE IT: harness/build_wasm.sh decoded exactly ONE frame and
    passed every one of those builds. That is the lesson worth more than the fix --
    a gate must exercise the shape the PRODUCT uses, not the smallest shape that
    proves the wire is connected. It now advances and renders 1200 frames.

    A bigger reserve buys frames and does not fix the shape. The fix is that the
    frame is PURE: nothing computed in it outlives the call, because the shim
    copies every coordinate into its own buffer before returning and the Codex
    side has no mutable state to carry over. So the region can be rewound to where
    it started and handed to the next frame:

        var cx_hp_base: i64 = 0;
        if (cx_hp_base == 0) cx_hp_base = cx_hp else cx_hp = cx_hp_base;

    Capture the base rather than writing 6291456, which is bare metal's own boot
    value for the heap pointer and mirrored by the plug -- see the deck note.

    THE SHIM IS THE ONLY PLACE THAT KNOWS A FRAME HAS ENDED. Nothing inside the
    transpiled program can know it; that is exactly the knowledge an arena needs,
    and it is why this belongs at the boundary rather than upstream.

C7. THE 4 GiB BUMP HEAP IS A CEILING ON HOW MUCH GOLD ONE UNIT CAN CARRY.

    Mountains' four frames are fifteen polygons of up to 683 points, which is
    16,868 coordinates. As a Codex list literal that is a 139 KB gold and a 187 KB
    unit, and codexzig dies transpiling it:

        thread panic: cx heap: exhausted at 4294926367 + 614587 of 4294967296

    Not a slow build -- an abort, from the same never-reclaiming bump region C6
    describes, hit by the TRANSPILER on its own input rather than by a transpiled
    program. Tree's 628 coordinates and a 43 KB unit are nowhere near it; the
    ceiling sits somewhere between.

    The fix that keeps the grading honest is to stride the COORDINATES and keep
    everything else: point counts stay exact, so a silhouette that gained or lost
    a column is still a hard failure, and what thins is only the sampling density
    of a curve that is smooth by construction. Every eighth point took the unit
    from 187 KB to 34 KB. Prefer that over dropping a frame -- the frames are the
    BRANCHES, and branch coverage is worth more than coordinate density.

C6. The bump heap never reclaims. Reserved once, bumped forever. Fine for one
    frame; a long-running process rebuilding a scene per frame will exhaust it.
    Design for one probe process per short horizon.


================================================================================
D. NUMERIC CALIBRATION — what the tolerances are actually measuring
================================================================================

D1. The f32/f64 gap is about 1.2e-7 relative, and that is the floor. The port
    computes in Real (f64); the game computes in f32; the gold reaches Codex as
    the shortest decimal that round-trips the f32, re-parsed at f64.

D2. Camera and Geom hold at 1e-6 RELATIVE over 375 values. Tightened to 1e-7,
    six of nine seams go red — so the gate sits one decade above the width gap
    and nothing more. It is calibrated, not slack.

D3. SCREEN COORDINATES NEED A MIXED GATE, atol + rtol*|want|. Both halves were
    measured, and each covers the other's failure:
      - relative fails at the horizon. A screen y is 300 minus a term of about
        the same size, so y = 20.6068 is a small DIFFERENCE OF TWO LARGE NUMBERS
        and cancellation amplifies the relative error by the 300/20.6 between
        them — 1.3e-6, about fifteen times f32 epsilon.
      - absolute fails at the near plane. A point 37 m to the side at 0.4 m
        forward projects to x = -62929, where ONE f32 ULP IS ALREADY 0.0075 px,
        seven times a thousandth-of-a-pixel floor.
    In use: atol 1e-3 px, rtol 1e-6. At a hundred pixels that is about a
    thousandth of a pixel, which is the precision worth asserting.

D5. A TOLERANCE FLOOR IS CALIBRATED PER SEAM, not copied from another one.

    D3 set the screen-coordinate gate at atol 1e-3 + rtol 1e-6 and that is right
    for the tree, whose coordinates run to -62929 where one f32 ulp is already
    0.0075 px. Sky's heights are screen pixels too and take the same SHAPE of
    gate, for the same cancellation reason -- but copying the 1e-3 floor with it
    would have been slack, and measurably so.

    The cancellation here is the worst yet seen. A sun height is 244 minus a term
    of nearly the same size, so with the disc's centre on the horizon it is
    244 - 243.993609 = 0.006391, and the f32/f64 gap there is:

        absolute   2.6e-6 px           nothing
        relative   4.1e-4              four hundred times a 1e-6 relative gate
        amplified  244/0.0064 = 38180x  (the tree's horizon case was 15x)

    So the floor has to admit 2.6e-6 and nothing else on that seam comes near
    zero. 1e-4 admits it with forty times to spare; 1e-3 sits four hundred times
    above the gap it exists for, and that is not free -- MEASURED: at 1e-3 a
    perturbation of sun-start-px by 2e-4 left sky-height GREEN while two other
    seams reddened. At 1e-4 the same perturbation reddens it.

    The rule: take the SHAPE of the gate from the failure mode, take the NUMBER
    from what that seam actually has to admit. A floor inherited from a seam with
    different magnitudes is a floor nobody calibrated.

D4. Structure is graded EXACTLY and must stay that way. Tags, colours and point
    counts take no tolerance. A wrong point count is a structural error and no
    coordinate tolerance should ever be able to hide one.

D6. THE CHAIN IS THE THIRD DISTINCT CANCELLATION, and the first in METRES.

    D3 and D5 both amplified at the screen: a coordinate near the horizon, a sun
    height near zero. Render's `at` does it in world space and for a different
    reason -- not because the answer is near zero, but because the COMPOSITION
    passes through coordinates a thousand times larger than the answer.

    Measured, at the rider's second chain segment, 160 m past its end:

        composed bottom-left frame   a = 1235.67 m, x = 56.91 m
        rider frame                  right = -1.5169 m
        right is                     -1115.7*sin(yaw) + 54.3*cos(yaw)
                                     two ~55 m terms that nearly cancel
        amplification                1235.7 / 1.52 = 815x
        absolute error               2.9e-5 m   (29 microns, a km away)
        relative error               1.9e-5     (20x every other metre seam)

    SO THE ERROR TRACKS THE LARGEST COORDINATE THE COMPOSITION TOUCHES, not the
    size of the answer -- that is what f32 has to hold on the way through. Which
    makes the floor a representation granularity and not a fudge factor, and it
    is set as one: one f32 ulp at the range the seam reaches.

        r-at    reaches 2015 m; one ulp there is 1.22e-4. Floor 2.5e-4 (two ulp).
                Reddens at 1e-4, passes at 1.2e-4.
        r-prev  composes ONE join, reaches 660 m; one ulp there is 6.1e-5.
                Floor 6e-5 (one ulp). Reddens at 1e-5, passes at 2e-5.

    The two differ because the two reaches differ, which is D5 applied rather
    than restated. Giving the behind joint the chain's floor would have been four
    times slacker than anything it needs.

    AND THE PORT'S OWN MATH IS NOT THE CAUSE. Recomputing `at` in f64 with the
    language's own @sin/@cos and comparing to the real f32 render.at gives the
    same picture -- 3.9e-7 at d = 0, 1.9e-5 at d = 2 -- so this is the f32/f64
    width gap amplified by the composition, and Trig is innocent. Note also that
    the error does NOT grow with depth: it peaks at d = 1 and d = 2 and falls
    away by d = 6. It is cancellation at particular geometry, not accumulation,
    so a deeper chain is not a worse chain.


================================================================================
E. WHAT A MODULE'S PRIVATE SURFACE DOES TO ITS CHECK
================================================================================

E1. RENDER.ZIG EXPOSES FOUR NAMES, AND THAT DECIDES HOW RENDER CAN BE GRADED.

    Its whole public surface is `Chain`, `Pose`, `at`, and `frame` (plus the two
    cull counters). buildChain, MapKind, Mapper, mapPt, emitGround,
    emitGroundColor, emitPondGround, emitJointGround, pushLeg and emitJointRail
    are all private to the file. A probe imports the module; it cannot reach any
    of them.

    Every other module in this port had a pub function per seam, so "the probe
    calls the real thing" was free. Here it is not, and the check has to say
    which of its rows is backed by what. RenderCheck grades three strengths:

      a REAL ORACLE      r-at. render.at is pub; the probe calls it.
      THE DEFINITION     r-prev. mapPt's prev branch is private, but it is
                         geom.curToNext then geom.toRider and both ARE pub, so
                         the probe composes the two real functions in that order.
                         Every arithmetic step is oracled; the ORDER is not.
      TRANSCRIBED        r-chain. buildChain is private AND `at` never consults
                         the caps -- hand `at` a chain of any length and it
                         composes it -- so the chain's EXTENT is not observable
                         through the public surface at all. LOOK_AHEAD (7) and
                         MAX_CHAIN (8) are copied from render.zig's source. Those
                         two integers are the only value in this port that is
                         transcribed rather than measured.

    THE WAY OUT IS ALREADY IN THE FILE, and it is worth taking when the
    collection lands. `render.cull_seg` IS pub, and frame() accumulates into it
    `n_cows + n_pigs` for every chain index at or beyond FARM_SEG_REACH. Both
    counts are graded exactly in WorldCheck. So one frame() call turns the
    chain's extent into a number the world's own graded data predicts: a chain
    one segment longer or shorter changes cull_seg by that segment's herd. That
    is a real oracle for the cap, built out of a counter that exists for
    instrumentation, and it closes the last transcribed value.

E2. CHECK WHAT IS `pub` BEFORE CHOOSING A SEAM, not after writing the port.

    The generalisation of E1. This port's seams have so far been chosen from the
    zig's own structure -- guard_rail's collect-then-draw, truck's motion-versus-
    body -- and that worked because those splits were also visibility splits. It
    is not guaranteed. Read the pub markers first; they are the real contract,
    and they can make a natural seam ungradeable and an awkward one free.
